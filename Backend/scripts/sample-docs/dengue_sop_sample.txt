Section 1.0 Clinical Definition and Disease Progression
Dengue fever is an acute mosquito-borne viral infection transmitted primarily by Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. The clinical course consists of three distinct phases:
1. Febrile Phase: Characterized by high fever (usually > 38.5°C), severe headache, retro-orbital pain, generalized arthralgia, and facial flushing lasting 2 to 7 days.
2. Critical Phase: Occurs around day 3 to 7 of illness, coinciding with defervescence. Characterized by systemic vascular leakage leading to hemoconcentration, thrombocytopenia, pleural effusion, and ascites.
3. Recovery Phase: Plasma leakage ceases, fluid is reabsorbed, and platelet count steadily recovers over 48 to 72 hours.

Section 2.0 Emergency Warning Signs and HDU Triage
Patients exhibiting any of the following warning signs must be immediately triaged for admission to the High Dependency Unit (HDU) or Intensive Care:
- Severe and persistent abdominal pain or tenderness.
- Persistent vomiting (> 3 episodes in 24 hours).
- Clinical fluid accumulation (ascites, pleural effusion).
- Spontaneous mucosal bleeding (epistaxis, gingival bleeding, hematuria, or melena).
- Lethargy, restlessness, or altered mental status.
- Hepatomegaly (> 2 cm below the right costal margin).
- Rapid drop in platelet count concurrent with hematocrit rise of > 20% from baseline.

Section 3.0 Fluid Resuscitation and Medical Management
Oral rehydration solution (ORS) and fruit juices are recommended in the febrile phase.
In patients with warning signs admitted to HDU:
- Initiate intravenous isotonic crystalloids (Normal Saline 0.9% or Ringer's Lactate) at 5 to 7 ml/kg/hr for 1-2 hours, then taper to 3 to 5 ml/kg/hr based on urine output and hematocrit stabilization.
- Maintain target urine output >= 0.5 ml/kg/hour.
- Avoid all Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs) such as Ibuprofen, Mefenamic Acid, and Aspirin due to the risk of severe gastrointestinal hemorrhage.
- Administer Paracetamol 500mg - 1000mg every 6 hours PRN for fever (maximum 3g/day in adults).

Section 4.0 Discharge Criteria
Patients can be safely discharged when ALL of the following criteria are met:
1. Afebrile for at least 48 hours without the use of antipyretics.
2. Resolution of warning signs and stable hemodynamic parameters.
3. Stable or increasing platelet count (> 50,000/μL) and normalized hematocrit without fluid boluses for 24 hours.
4. Good appetite and normal bowel/bladder function.
